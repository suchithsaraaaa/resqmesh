## Chapter 1: Heat Wave Alert Thresholds & Inter-Agency Preparedness
**Section:** Heat Wave Alert Thresholds & Inter-Agency Preparedness | **Page Reference:** 10

India Meteorological Department (IMD) heat wave alert criteria:
1. Plains Criteria: Maximum temperature reaches at least 40°C or departure from normal is 4.5°C to 6.4°C (Heat Wave), and >6.4°C departure (Severe Heat Wave).
2. Coastal & Hilly Criteria: Maximum temperature reaches 37°C (coastal) or 30°C (hilly regions).
3. Color-Coded Warning Levels:
   - Yellow Alert (Watch): Normal heat alert, monitor vulnerable populations.
   - Orange Alert (Alert): Moderate heat wave continuing for 2+ days; high risk for infants, elderly, chronic illness patients.
   - Red Alert (Warning): Severe heat wave continuing for 2+ days or total days >4; extreme health hazard, heat stroke imminent.
4. Shift Operations for Laborers & Field Responders: Mandate halt of all outdoor physical labor between 12:00 PM and 3:30 PM under Orange/Red alerts.

## Chapter 2: Heat Stroke Recognition, Emergency Cooling & Responder Safety
**Section:** Heat Stroke Recognition, Emergency Cooling & Responder Safety | **Page Reference:** 24

Heat stroke is a medical emergency with mortality exceeding 50% without immediate treatment:
1. Clinical Symptoms: Core body temperature > 40°C (104°F), altered mental status (confusion, delirium, seizures, coma), hot dry skin or profuse sweating.
2. Immediate Evaporative Cooling Protocol: Move casualty immediately to shaded air-conditioned area. Strip outer clothing. Spray body continuously with tepid water (20-25°C) while directing high-speed electric fans across torso.
3. Ice Pack Application: Place sealed ice packs on high-vascular zones: neck, armpits (axillae), and groin. Cease active cooling when core temperature drops to 38.5°C (101°F) to prevent hypothermic overshoot.
4. Rehydration Rules: Conscious patients: Administer oral rehydration salt (ORS) solution slowly. Unconscious patients: NEVER administer oral fluids (aspiration risk); establish IV access and infuse chilled Normal Saline.
5. Responder Work-to-Rest Cycles: Responders in protective gear must drink 1 liter of cool water per hour and take 15-minute rest breaks every 45 minutes in designated shaded cooling shelters.
